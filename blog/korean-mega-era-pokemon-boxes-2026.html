Value Guide

Korean prints of the same set typically run at roughly 60–80% of the equivalent Japanese box price. Japanese market prices are the clearest benchmark available, so use them as a ceiling, not a target. Check eBay sold listings or Korean platforms like Bunjang for current comps before you buy or trade.

M2 — Inferno X (인페르노X)

Cover: Mega Charizard X

The most expensive box in the MEGA series right now. Charizard demand is evergreen — any sealed Charizard product holds its floor well. At 30 packs × 5 cards, it's a solid break box with real upside.

M2a — MEGA Dream ex (MEGA 드림ex)

Cover: Mega Gengar | High-Class Pack

10 packs × 10 cards, with a Pokémon ex or Mega ex guaranteed in every pack. Chase cards include Mega Gengar ex SAR, Mega Dragonite ex SAR and MUR, and N's Zoroark ex SAR. The hit rate makes this a crowd-pleaser for breaks.

M5 — Abyss Eye (어비스아이)

Cover: Darkrai

The entry point of the four. Cheapest Japanese box on the market right now, and the Korean version follows suit. Still has a Mega ex at MUR rarity. Best value pick if budget is a factor.

M6 — Storm Emeralda (스톰에메랄다)

Cover: Rayquaza

The newest of the four, printed August 2026. Rayquaza always draws strong collector interest — it's one of the most popular Dragon types in the game. Fresh sealed stock on a new set with early price momentum.

Collector tip: Korean hobby cards can vary in centering run to run. Check condition on any hits before submitting for grading, especially on lower-pop cards where condition premium is significant.

Are These Legit?

Yes. All four are Pokémon Korea prints with KC certification, 880028627-series barcodes, and branded factory shrink wrap — standard authentication for Korean hobby boxes.
