How to Evaluate Your Pokémon Collection Before Selling

By Elburn Card Shop · September 2026 · 5 min read

So you've got a box of Pokémon cards sitting in the closet — maybe from your childhood, maybe from a recent hobby run — and you're wondering what it's worth. Before you walk into any card shop (including ours), a little preparation will get you a better offer and a smoother experience. Here's exactly how to do it.

Step 1: Sort First, Count Second

The number one mistake sellers make is showing up with an unsorted pile. Sorting takes 20 minutes and can meaningfully change your offer. Separate your cards into three piles:

  • Holos and rares — anything with a shiny or textured front, ex cards, V cards, VMAX, full arts
  • Uncommons and commons — the bulk of most collections
  • Damaged or heavily played — creases, water damage, heavy scratches

Bulk (commons/uncommons in good condition) has a per-card value, and holos/rares are evaluated individually. Keeping them mixed slows down the process for everyone.

Step 2: Identify the High-Value Cards

Not sure what's rare? Look for these indicators on the card itself:

  • A star (★) or multiple stars in the bottom-right corner
  • Cards labeled "ex", "V", "VMAX", "VSTAR", "GX", or "EX"
  • Full-art or alternate-art illustrations (the art bleeds to the edge)
  • Gold or rainbow holographic cards
  • Anything with a crown symbol (Crown Rare)

Once you've pulled your standout cards, run a quick search on TCGplayer.com or look up recent eBay sold listings. Filter by the card name + set + condition. This gives you a realistic baseline before you walk in.

💡 Pro tip: TCGplayer shows "market price" — the actual average of recent sales. That's more reliable than the listed prices, which can be inflated.

Step 3: Be Honest About Condition

Condition is everything in this hobby. A Charizard in mint condition might be worth 10x what the same card in poor condition fetches. Standard grading tiers used in the hobby:

  • Near Mint (NM): Essentially perfect — no visible wear, sharp corners
  • Lightly Played (LP): Minor edge wear, very slight scratches — still clean
  • Moderately Played (MP): Visible scratches, edge wear, light creasing
  • Heavily Played (HP): Significant wear, creases, possible water damage
  • Damaged (DMG): Major issues — tears, heavy bends, writing

Most shop buy prices assume Lightly Played or better. If your cards are MP or below, expect a lower offer — that's not a lowball, it's the reality of what those cards can sell for.

Step 4: Know What to Expect From a Buy Offer

Card shops pay wholesale rates — typically 40–60% of market value on desirable singles, less on bulk. This isn't a rip-off; it's how retail works. The shop has to cover overhead, sit on inventory, and take the risk that the card doesn't sell quickly.

If a card's market price is $50, a fair shop offer is $20–$30 cash, or potentially more in store credit. Trade-in credit usually gets you 10–20% more than cash.

Step 5: Consider Grading for Your Best Cards

If you have cards in true Near Mint or better condition from desirable sets — think Base Set Charizard, vintage holos, or modern alt arts — it may be worth getting them professionally graded before selling. A PSA 10 on the right card can be worth 3–5x the raw card price.
