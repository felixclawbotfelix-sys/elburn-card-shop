2025 Topps Chrome Football Is Here — What to Know Before You Buy

September 2026  ·  5 min read  ·  Elburn Card Shop

Every fall, collectors circle one date on their calendar more than any other in football cards: the release of Topps Chrome Football. The 2025 edition is now available, and if you've been sitting on the fence, here's everything you need to know before you rip — or buy a sealed box as an investment.

Why Chrome Football Is the Hobby's Gold Standard

Topps Chrome has been the benchmark football card product for over 25 years. The reason is simple: the technology. Chrome cards use a reflective foil stock that photographs better, holds condition better, and — critically — grades better. A PSA 10 Chrome rookie card typically sells for 2–5x more than the same card in Topps base.

For 2025, Topps continued its winning formula: one hobby box contains 18 packs with 4 cards each (72 cards total), with guaranteed autographs and refractors that drive the real value.

Sealed Box vs. Singles: Which Is the Better Play?

This is the most common question we get at the shop. Here's the honest answer:

  • Sealed boxes are best when you want the experience of the break and you understand you may not "hit." Think of it like a lottery ticket — thrilling, but you're paying for the fun as much as the value.
  • Singles are better for targeted value. If you know you want a Travis Hunter Chrome auto, buying the specific card is almost always cheaper than boxes needed to find one.
  • Graded slabs (PSA, BGS, CGC) are the best long-term investment — but require patience (grading turnaround + holding time).

Should You Grade Your Pulls?

If you pull a numbered Chrome auto of a top rookie, grading is almost always worth it — the premium for a PSA 10 vs. raw is substantial. For base Chrome rookies, only grade if you have genuine gem mint candidates (perfect centering, no print lines, clean edges). Grading a $5 card for $25 doesn't make math sense.
